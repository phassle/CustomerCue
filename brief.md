**CustomerCue**

**One-Liner:**  
CustomerCue finds churn risk, expansion signals, and product friction inside B2B SaaS support conversations.

**Tagline:**  
**Turn support conversations into customer revenue signals.**

**Category:**  
AI revenue intelligence for Customer Success, Support, and Product teams.

**Positioning:**  
CustomerCue is not another AI support bot. It is an intelligence layer on top of Intercom, Zendesk, and similar support systems that turns customer conversations into prioritized business signals.

**Core Insight:**  
The support inbox often knows first which customers are unhappy, which accounts are ready to expand, which product issues create friction, and which bugs affect high-value customers. But those signals usually stay buried in tickets, tags, and individual agents’ heads.

**ICP:**  
B2B SaaS companies with:

- 50-500 employees
- recurring revenue
- support handled in Intercom or Zendesk
- a Customer Success team or account managers
- enough support volume for patterns to emerge
- churn, retention, and expansion as leadership priorities

**Primary Buyer:**  
VP Customer Success.

**Secondary Buyers:**  
Head of Support, COO, CRO, VP Product.

**Daily Users:**  
Support leads, CSMs, account managers, and product managers.

**Problem:**  
Companies already have thousands of customer conversations, but they mostly use them to resolve individual tickets. They lack a system that continuously answers:

- Which customers are at risk of churn?
- Which customers are showing expansion intent?
- Which bugs affect important accounts?
- Which feature requests come from high-value customers?
- Which onboarding problems keep repeating?
- Which topics are driving support volume?
- Which accounts need CSM action this week?

**Product Promise:**  
CustomerCue reads the support flow, connects it to customer and revenue data, and turns conversations into prioritized actions.

**First Product Version:**  

- Intercom or Zendesk integration
- Import of historical tickets/conversations
- Customer data import through CSV, HubSpot, or Salesforce
- Basic mapping to ARR, plan, segment, and account owner
- AI classification of conversations
- Dashboard with risks, opportunities, and recurring themes
- Slack/email alerts to the right owner
- Weekly report for CS, Product, and Support leadership

**Signal Types:**  

- Churn risk
- Expansion intent
- Product friction
- Bug cluster
- Onboarding issue
- Feature request
- Negative sentiment
- Strategic account escalation
- Documentation gap
- Repeated manual workaround

**Example Outputs:**  
“Acme Corp, $42k ARR, has opened 6 tickets in 14 days about the same integration issue. Sentiment is declining. CSM should contact the customer before the renewal call.”

“NordicPay has repeatedly asked about SSO, audit logs, and admin roles. This likely indicates enterprise expansion intent.”

“37 tickets from 11 customers relate to onboarding step 3. Four of those customers are in the target ICP. Product should prioritize this ahead of lower-value feature requests.”

**Differentiation:**  
Intercom Fin and Zendesk AI focus heavily on answering and resolving support issues. CustomerCue focuses on what support conversations mean for revenue, retention, and product priorities. Gainsight is moving toward agentic retention workflows, but CustomerCue can win with a faster, narrower, support-native wedge.

**MVP Wedge:**  
Do not start with an autonomous support agent. Start with:

**“Weekly revenue signals from your support inbox.”**

This is easier to sell, easier to trust, and easier to validate.

**Pricing Hypothesis:**  

- Starter: EUR 499-999/month, limited conversation volume
- Growth: EUR 1,500-3,000/month, CRM + Slack + dashboards
- Enterprise: EUR 5,000+/month, SSO, audit logs, custom taxonomy, security review

**Why Now:**  
The AI support market is moving quickly toward automation. Intercom is pushing Fin as an AI agent, Zendesk is expanding AI agent capabilities, and CS platforms like Gainsight are moving retention toward agentic workflows. That makes the market ready, but it also exposes the gap: many vendors optimize resolution, while few own revenue signals hidden in support conversations.

**Main Risks:**  

- Intercom/Zendesk build this natively
- The product becomes a passive dashboard instead of a workflow
- Classification creates too much noise
- Customers do not trust AI signals without clear sources
- ROI is hard to prove if alerts do not lead to action
- Supporting too many inboxes early slows the first version down

**Mitigations:**  

- Always show the source conversations behind every signal
- Start with Intercom or Zendesk, not every support inbox
- Focus on B2B SaaS, not all customer service
- Push signals into existing workflows: Slack, CRM, CSM tasks
- Measure closed loop: signal -> action -> outcome

**Validation Plan:**  
The first test should be a manual or AI-assisted report.

Offer:  
**“Send us 1,000 support conversations. We return your churn, expansion, and product-risk report in 48 hours.”**

The goal is to make the customer say:  
“We should receive this every week.”

**Success Criteria:**  

- 3-5 companies agree to test with real data
- at least 2 say the report found something they had missed
- at least 1 wants to pay for a recurring version
- signals lead to real CSM/Product/Sales actions
- the buyer can describe ROI in churn reduction, expansion, or saved analysis time

**Open Questions:**  

- Should the first integration be Intercom or Zendesk?
- Is the best first buyer VP CS or Head of Support?
- Should the first output be a dashboard, Slack alerts, or a weekly report?
- Which signal is most valuable first: churn, expansion, or product friction?
- How much CRM data is required to create clear value?
- What security level is required before customers share support data?

**Recommendation:**  
Build the company around **CustomerCue**. The first product should not be an “AI support agent.” It should be **support conversation intelligence for revenue teams**. The first sellable wedge is a recurring report/alert product that surfaces churn risk, expansion signals, and product friction from real support conversations.
